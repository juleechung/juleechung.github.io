The project began with initial meetings with professors [Zhi Tan](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/people/zhi-tan/) and [Stacy C. Marsella](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/people/stacy-c-marsella/) to define the research direction. We framed the central question around how a robot should interpret human instructions that combine speech, gesture, spatial context, and social cues.

The first week was mainly about narrowing the scope. Multimodal HRI is broad, and the most important early decision was to focus on deictic instructions: phrases like "this," "that," and "over there" that only make sense when they are grounded in the physical scene. This gave the project a concrete path: study how a robot might connect a spoken command, a pointing gesture, and its camera view before deciding whether to act or ask a clarifying question.

I also began thinking about possible domain applications. Museums remained a useful long-term context because they are public, object-rich, and socially complex, but the immediate research problem became more general: how can a robot understand situated human communication in a shared environment?
