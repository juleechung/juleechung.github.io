I added verbal command support and began integrating speech into the grounding workflow. The system moved from typed instructions toward voice input, spoken responses, and typed-or-spoken answers to clarifying questions.

This changed the interaction loop substantially. With speech enabled, the robot can capture a command such as "move this over there," transcribe it with [faster-whisper](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper) (the `base` model, CPU, int8), combine it with gesture evidence, and decide whether the fused confidence is high enough to act. If confidence is low, the system can ask a follow-up question and update its belief after the human answers.

I also made the GUI more robust around recording windows, microphone selection, and fallback behavior. These details matter because an HRI study can fail for mundane reasons: the microphone is wrong, the robot misses the gesture timing, or the operator cannot tell whether the system is listening. The goal was to make the interaction legible enough to run repeatedly.
