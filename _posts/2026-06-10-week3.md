I continued the literature review with a closer look at deictic gestures and [Herbert Clark](https://web.stanford.edu/~clark/index.html)'s work on language use, common ground, and joint action. I also began attending [CESAR Lab](https://www.cesarlab.org/) meetings to better understand related work on social behavior, cognition, and interaction.

Clark's [*Using Language*](https://scholar.google.com/citations?view_op=view_citation&hl=de&user=0jeEp_sAAAAJ&citation_for_view=0jeEp_sAAAAJ:u5HHmVD_uO8C) was useful for thinking beyond command following. In human-human interaction, people do not simply issue and execute instructions; they establish common ground, check evidence, repair misunderstandings, and work toward joint closure. That lens made clarifying dialogue feel central rather than optional.

This week helped shift the project from "can the model identify the pointed object?" to a broader question: what should a robot do when it has partial evidence? A socially grounded robot should be able to recognize when an instruction is under-specified, ask a useful follow-up question, and make its interpretation legible before acting.
