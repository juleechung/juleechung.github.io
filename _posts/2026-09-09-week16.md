I refined the benchmark visualisation so every model's answer sits on one frame. The overlay marks where each model points, whether it answered directly or asked a question, and where my own [pipeline](https://github.com/juleechung/multimodal_hri/tree/main/ablation_YOLO) put its box.

I gave one frame to four systems. In the frame, I'm pointing at three similar bottles on the floor, and the given command is "pick that up". Each system ran three times, once with no constraint on the answer, once told to return a coordinate, and once told it could ask a clarifying question instead.

![Four models answering the instruction pick that up on one frame, with their chosen points overlaid]({{ site.baseurl }}/images/deictic-grounding-model-comparison.jpg)

*Every answer to "pick that up", overlaid on the one frame.*

My own system combines YOLO-Worldv2 semantic object detection and YOLOv8n-pose pointing geometry, producing a selected referent with a fused confidence score.

The clarifying condition is where the four systems separate. Gemini Robotics-ER asked which of the three bottles on the floor I meant. That is the same move my own pipeline makes when it says it sees three different bottles and asks which one I would like. Mine arrives there by counting detections inside a pointing cone. Gemini arrives there from pixels alone. GPT-5 and Molmo2-ER never ask and always return a coordinate.

The ground truth is the bottle furthest from me, on the left of the frame. GPT-5 and Molmo2-ER both landed on it in the two conditions that told them to use the pointing gesture. In the free condition, where nothing directed them to the gesture, both chose a different bottle. Gemini Robotics-ER asked a question in two conditions and gave one coordinate, and that coordinate was wrong. My own pipeline also picked the wrong bottle, the same one Gemini picked.

The most useful result is that the pointing instruction is what moved GPT-5 and Molmo2-ER from wrong to right. The image frame and the models were the same in every condition, so being told that the gesture matters is what made the difference.

Interestingly, my own model, where the gesture is built into the pipeline as geometry, still chose the wrong bottle.

Gemini answered in six to nine seconds and GPT-5 in seven to seventeen. Molmo2-ER took sixty-five to seventy-eight seconds. Molmo2-ER is the only one of the three that runs offline, and unless the compute changes it would be very slow to use on a live robot.

### Model reasoning

The adapters return the reasoning each model produced alongside its answer. Molmo2-ER returns a coordinate with no reasoning.

The Gemini point trace was interesting, because it describes extracting shoulder, elbow and wrist, computing the slope, extending the vector, and concluding the white can. It did the geometry in prose and still landed on the wrong bottle, which sits alongside my own pipeline doing the same geometry numerically and making the same mistake.
