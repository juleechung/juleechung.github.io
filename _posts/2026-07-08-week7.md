I explored whether Spot simulation in [MuJoCo](https://mujoco.org/) would be useful for the project. I set up the Spot arm model from [MuJoCo Menagerie](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco_menagerie), added camera views, placed a target object in the scene, and ran an early visual grounding test.

![Spot with its arm extended, standing in the MuJoCo physics viewer]({{ site.baseurl }}/images/spot-mujoco-viewer.png)

*Spot with the arm loaded from MuJoCo Menagerie's `scene_arm.xml`, standing in the physics viewer.*

MuJoCo was useful for understanding robot assets, camera placement, and basic simulation setup. I learned that the DeepMind Menagerie Spot model did not include the camera configuration I needed by default, so I added six body-mounted cameras and settled on a forward-facing one as the primary egocentric view. I then placed a tilted red box in the scene and tested a simple instruction, "straighten that out."

![Egocentric camera view in which Spot's own arm occludes the red target box]({{ site.baseurl }}/images/spot-mujoco-egocentric-view.png)

*The egocentric view the model actually receives. Spot's own arm runs down the centre of the frame and cuts across the red target box.*

The first grounding test used [LLaVA](https://ollama.com/library/llava) served locally through Ollama, chosen because it is lightweight enough to run offline. LLaVA read the action correctly and asked a clarifying question at 70% confidence, but misidentified Spot's own arm as the referent:

> 1. The human is referring to the image of a gun, which appears distorted or out of focus in the robot's camera view.
>
> 2. "Straighten that out" means to align or correct the orientation of an object so it is perpendicular to the ground and pointing straight up or down. In this context, it likely refers to adjusting the gun's position so that it is properly oriented for use.
>
> 3. The robot should identify the camera's current perspective relative to the gun, calculate the necessary angle correction based on the desired orientation (either vertically or horizontally), and then physically manipulate the gun to align with the correct orientation. This may involve rotating the gun along its vertical axis if it is currently not aligned properly with respect to gravity.
>
> 4. I am confident at 70%. To clarify, I would need more information about the current position of the gun and the desired orientation for it to achieve a precise solution.

This made the limitation of simulation clear. The question I am studying is physically and socially involved: it depends on real camera distortion, human gesture, robot embodiment, object occlusion, and the back-and-forth of clarification. Simulation was helpful for orientation, but the project needed to move toward a physical experiment with Spot.
