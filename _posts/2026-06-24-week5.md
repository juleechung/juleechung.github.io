This week I tested frontier models on deictic gestures in construction-style scenes. The scenarios included material transport, alignment or leveling, and surface treatment tasks such as "move these to that corner," "straighten that out," and "paint that wall."

The construction examples were helpful because they made ambiguity practical rather than abstract. A robot might need to distinguish between an object and a destination, understand that "straighten" implies a current misalignment, or ask whether a wall instruction includes trim, doors, fixtures, or blocked surfaces. In several cases, the best response was not immediate action but a targeted clarifying question.

This work also suggested a useful evaluation structure: vary the available context. A model might receive only a photo, a photo plus orientation text, a floor plan, or video with gesture. Comparing those conditions made it easier to think about which input channels actually improve grounding.
