I began working physically with Spot using the [Boston Dynamics Spot SDK](https://github.com/boston-dynamics/spot-sdk). The focus was getting reliable access to Spot's camera streams, testing basic robot commands, and understanding the safety workflow around leases, power, and E-Stop behavior.

![Spot's five body cameras streaming simultaneously in a labelled grid]({{ site.baseurl }}/images/spot-camera-grid.gif)

*Spot's five body cameras recording at once: front-left, front-right, left, right, and rear. The same person passes through different panes as they move, which is why grounding a pointing gesture depends on which camera happens to see it.*

The early physical work changed the project in an important way. Working with Spot made it clear that grounding cannot be separated from the robot's actual sensor layout: front, side, rear, and gripper cameras each provide a different partial view of the scene. I started testing how camera images could be captured, displayed, recorded, and passed into a vision-language model.

This week also made safety and usability part of the system design. Before the robot can execute anything, the interface needs clear controls for power, E-Stop, recording, and operator confirmation. That led to a more careful plan for a GUI-based experiment rather than a script-only pipeline.

The code from this point onward lives in [multimodal_hri](https://github.com/juleechung/multimodal_hri), the repository this project grew into.
