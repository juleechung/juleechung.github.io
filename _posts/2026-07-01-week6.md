I spent this week examining different robot platforms and learning more about what types of robots are suited to different HRI tasks. This helped separate the research question from any one piece of hardware.

[Spot](https://bostondynamics.com/products/spot/) is useful for this project because it can operate in shared physical spaces and has multiple camera views, mobility, and an arm/gripper configuration; the [academic kit specifications](https://bostondynamics.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/spot-academia-kit.pdf) list the full sensor and payload options. At the same time, other platforms raise different questions: a tabletop manipulator is better for controlled grasping tasks, while a mobile service robot may be better for navigation and social interaction studies.

![Boston Dynamics Spot fitted with an arm and gripper and a sensor payload on its back]({{ site.baseurl }}/images/spot-with-arm.jpg)

This comparison helped clarify why Spot is an interesting testbed for deictic HRI. The problem is not only recognizing a gesture; it is grounding that gesture from a robot's embodied perspective, deciding whether to move for a better view, and communicating clearly before taking physical action.
